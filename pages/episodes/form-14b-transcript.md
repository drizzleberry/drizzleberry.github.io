## Section 5. Storage

**Location of Retrieval:** \[Jamie's Handwriting:\] Initially discovered by me (Jamie) in a heap of boxes in aisle 17, in a small cardboard box. The box had signs of wear indicating it was several years old, plus it appeared to be sealed through unknown means, as there was no sign of packaging tape or staples holding it shut. The box had the words "#847" written on it, which has been the cause of much speculation.

**Current Storage Location:** \[Jamie's Handwriting:\] (Other) Currently not stored in an aisle/bay. It is leaned against the far wall

**Special Storage Requirements:**

\[Jamie's Handwriting:\] Object 847 is currently placed in a large plastic barrel. The item itself is in a plastic jug (duct taped to the side) with a hole cut in the bottom that allows its liquid to flow into the barrel without losing the object in its own goo. The barrel is said to hold about 200 liters. The barrel must be emptied every two days to prevent it from overflowing. (this is ideally done during the day shift when multiple employees are on duty) We currently rotate between two barrels, which gives us enough time to empty the first one into a larger contianer that should be sent to be handled by someone else.

Keep in mind that this is only a temporary solution, until we can (hopefully) come up with a more effective and safe long-term solution.

**Disposal Considerations:**

\[Jamie's Handwriting:\] We decided as a staff body to avoid disposing of the object for now, as we weren't able to reach a conclusion on how to correctly dispose of it. Some ideas included: throwing it in the trash (decided to be unsafe), calling the HWCA (overruled). Because it constantly produces fluid, there haven't been any satisfying conclusions on how to get rid of it without causing a huge catastrophe later.

The current plan is to take the liquid waste and ship it to a waste management company. They are thinking of sending it to one that is nearby, they should hopefully not ask too many questions. The object should however be kept here for safety.
